# capable v3: security, contract and reliability review

Independent reviewer. Fresh clone `/tmp/review-v3sec` at `d391dbc`. Nothing in the repo was changed. Scratch work (adversarial contract tests, probes, mutation harness) is in `/tmp/review-v3sec-scratch/`. Transactions went to local anvil only. Read-only RPC to `mainnet.base.org` is blocked in this sandbox, so the live contract's state was not inspected. The local build reproduces the recorded deploy `codeHash` (`0x8e0d…a4b1`, 14,407 bytes).

## Verdict

The contract is sound. Every transfer and approve path reverts, both as an eth_call and as a mined tx (all five selectors, even when the holder sends them). Mint uses `_mint`, so there is no receiver hook and nothing to re-enter. A 60-step random mint/burn fuzz kept `tokensOf`, `hasStamp`, `balanceOf` and the seeds consistent. ERC-165/5192 are correct. Metadata injection is impossible: names are `[A-Z ]` and colours are hex. The worst-case `tokenURI` is about 523k gas. Supply exhaustion would take about 1.15e14 gas, so it is infeasible.

Identity verification is correct where it matters (nonce, domain, fid↔signer, expiry). The identity is display-only and nothing security-relevant trusts it. The workflow gate holds up well:
- Fork PRs cannot merge.
- The merge is pinned to the checked SHA.
- Symlinks, gitlinks, executables and odd paths are refused.
- The four action SHAs match their tags (checked with `git ls-remote`).

The real problems are in the product and the flows around the contract:
- **(V1)** The mint flow cannot tell a reverted or dropped tx from a mint. The app also ignores the on-chain `open` flag, so the owner's only bot lever (closing a trait) turns into reverted, gas-burning txs that the app records as "sent" forever.
- **(V2)** The mint is free and ungated, so stamps are farmable at about $0.01 per wallet and prove nothing beyond "this address clicked mint". The contract is immutable, so this is a product decision, not a patch (see "Bot minting: options").
- The stamps lane's own test and gate are red at HEAD **(V3)**.
- The editor's auto-merge publishes a combination of files that was never validated **(V4)**, and unreviewed external links **(V5)**.

No blocker.

Counts: **0 blocker · 2 major · 10 minor · 13 nit**.

Adversarial chain results:
- **A1 soulbound:** all 5 transfer/approve selectors revert as mined txs, even from the holder. Value sends and unknown selectors revert.
- **A2 no receiver hook:** a contract that reverts in `onERC721Received` still mints. No callback, no reentrancy.
- **A3 id burn-through:** a mint+burn loop from **one** address costs **115,352 gas per id**. Exhausting `MAX_TOKEN_ID` takes about 1.15e14 gas: 115–577 ETH at 0.001–0.005 gwei, and weeks of Base's entire capacity. Not a practical brick.
- **A4 farming:** a factory makes **20 holders × 4 stamps in one tx**, at 664k gas per farmed holder (≈ $0.01).
- **A5 fuzz:** 60 random mint/burn steps across 3 holders. `tokensOf` = live set, `balanceOf`, `hasStamp` and `seedOf = keccak(holder, stampId, id)` all hold.
- **A6 calldata:** a dirty `uint8` (256) reverts. Burning id 0, a missing id, or another holder's id reverts `NotHolder`, the owner included.
- **A7 tokenURI worst case:** a 12-letter name, pixel emblem and serial 999,999,999 estimates at **523,140 gas**. It succeeds under a 1M-gas eth_call cap; the URI is 4,665 chars.
- **A8 renounce:** after `renounceOwnership`, `setOpen` and `addTrait` revert and traits stay open forever (V18).

## Findings

### Major

**V1: A reverted or never-mined mint is recorded as "sent" forever, and the app ignores the trait's on-chain `open` flag.** Owner: app (mint.mjs) + stamps.
- **Where:**
  - `app/src/stamps/index.mjs:69`: `stampTrait()` hardcodes `open: true`, and nothing in `app/src` reads `traitOf`.
  - `app/src/stamps/index.mjs:184-195`: there is no `hasStamp(from, stampId)` check after `eth_requestAccounts`.
  - `app/src/mint.mjs:121-126`: any returned hash is passed to `recordMint` as "sent".
  - `app/src/mint.mjs:50-53`: once "sent", the mint is never offered again in this browser.
  - Nothing ever calls `eth_getTransactionReceipt`.
- **Repro:** `node /tmp/review-v3sec-scratch/chain/closed-trait.probe.mjs`. On anvil (chain 8453) the owner calls `setOpen(0,false)`, and a wallet that lets the person "proceed anyway" (as MetaMask and Coinbase do after a failed simulation) sends. Output:
  - `mint() resolved … → receipt status: reverted (trait 0 closed)`
  - `second mint of a held stamp resolved too; receipt: reverted`
- **Effects:**
  - Closing a trait (the only anti-bot lever, see below) makes the app offer a mint that burns gas and then shows "Sent. It joins your passport…" with a link to a failed tx.
  - The same happens for a stamp minted earlier from another browser when `eth_accounts` was empty at card time, so `owned()` never ran.
  - A tx that was dropped or underpriced also leaves the trait stuck at "sent".
- **Fix:**
  - Read `traitOf(stampId).open` in `look()` (one eth_call) and show "closed" instead of mint.
  - After `eth_requestAccounts`, `eth_call hasStamp(from, stampId)` → "held".
  - Record `{txHash}` as "pending". Poll `eth_getTransactionReceipt` on mainnet.base.org (already in `connect-src`). Mark "minted" on success, and clear the record (offer the mint again) on a revert, or on no receipt after about 10 min.

**V2: The mint is free and ungated, so stamps are farmed and carry no signal.** Owner: stamps + planner.
- **Where:** `chain/src/CapableStamps.sol:81-100`.
- **Measured (A4):** about 650k gas per EOA for all four stamps. A factory produces 80 stamps per tx. Both cost about $0.01 per farmed holder at Base's usual prices. 21 bot wallets minted within minutes of the deploy.
- **Docs:** the contract and README say "honor system", which is honest. `docs/SPEC-V3.md` ("so anyone can verify it") overclaims: on chain, a stamp proves only that an address called `mint`.
- **What it doesn't do:** it puts no one's funds at risk and costs the project nothing; the bots pay the gas. It does destroy any meaning in holder counts or stamp ownership, and a public passport (`#/p/<addr>`) can't tell a reader from a bot.
- **Fix:** see "Bot minting: options". The v1 contract is immutable.

### Minor

**V3: The stamps lane test and gate are red at HEAD.** Owner: stamps.
- **Where:** `chain/test/deploy.test.mjs:195` asserts `parseTraitArgs(["open","1",…])` throws "no contract address", because "app.meta.json ships with address null". Commit `a6a1ac5` set the address.
- **Effect:** `npm test` in `chain/` fails 1/31, and `gate --lane stamps` prints GATE FAIL. That is the owner's runbook step 1 (DEPLOY-STAMPS §1/§6). No workflow runs `chain/` tests, so CI never noticed.
- **Fix:** run that assertion against a temp copy of `app.meta.json` with `address: null`, and add chain tests to a workflow (paths `chain/**`).

**V4: Editor auto-merge squashes a combination that was never validated.** Owner: ingest.
- **Where:** `.github/workflows/editor.yml:36-53` validates `refs/pull/N/merge` against main **at check time**. `editor-merge.yml:58-76` then squash-merges onto main **at merge time**, with only path and metadata checks.
- **Effect:** if main moved in between (a refresh commit changed `book.json`, or a human or second editor PR merged a lesson with the same `number`, `id` or `topic`), the merged tree was never run through `--strict-lessons`. `deploy.yml` compiles non-strict, so the conflicting lesson is **silently dropped** at deploy (`compile-app.mjs:272-286`), with only a log warning.
- **Fix:** in `editor-merge.yml`, before the merge:
  - require `pull.base.sha` == current `origin/main`, or re-run main's own `validate-app.mjs --strict` and `refresh.mjs --offline --strict-lessons` on main's checkout with the PR's lesson/works files laid over it (`git checkout $HEAD_SHA -- content/lessons content/seed/works.json`). That is main's code reading data, not PR code;
  - update the `refresh.test.mjs:512` assertion that forbids `validate-app` in the merge job.

**V5: Auto-merged lessons can add links to any https host with no human review, and the app now has a wallet flow.** Owner: ingest/planner.
- **Where:** `contract/validate-app.mjs:72` allows any https `sources[].url`, and `editor-check.mjs` checks only paths.
- **Exposure:** the editor follows web-search results and network-derived `book.json` text. A prompt-injected session can cite a "claim your stamp" or drainer page, and it ships with no review. The links open with `noopener`, but they appear on a card the reader trusts.
- **Fix:** hold any editor PR that introduces a source host not already in the lessons (or not on an allow-list) for a person. For example, `editor-check.mjs pr` could diff source hosts and exit 1, so the PR waits for review.

**V6: The editor gate depends on credentials and rulesets that are not in the repo (residual S16).** Owner: planner/owner.
- **PR-time workflow is the PR's:** `editor.yml` runs from the **PR merge commit**, so a same-repo PR can rewrite it at check time and read any repository/org secrets before `editor-merge` refuses the paths. The editor uses the owner's GitHub connector (REFRESH-PROMPT step 6), and `EDITOR_LOGIN` is presumably the owner's login, so the session holds full push rights. "Never push to main" is enforced only by the prompt.
- **Fix:**
  - Give the editor a dedicated bot account or a fine-grained token **without the Workflows permission**. GitHub then refuses pushes that touch `.github/workflows/`, even on branches.
  - Set `EDITOR_LOGIN` to that bot.
  - Add a ruleset on `main` (block force-push and direct pushes except by the owner and `github-actions`).
  - Keep repository secrets empty.
- **Related:** V25.

**V7: After a code deploy, open sessions can't load lazy chunks; sign-in, mint and video fail until a reload.** Owner: app.
- **Where:** `app/lib/sw.mjs:29-44`.
- **What happens:**
  1. The new SW runs `skipWaiting()`, then `clients.claim()`, and deletes every older `capable:<scope>:*` cache, including lazy chunks cached on first use.
  2. Pages has already replaced the old hashed files.
  3. A page still running old JS (a resumed standalone PWA, or a second tab) does `import("./flows.OLD.js")`, gets a 404, and fails with `network` (identity) or `offline` (stamps). "Try again" repeats the same 404.
- **Fix:**
  - On a dynamic-import failure, compare against the live `sw.js` / `index.html` and `location.reload()` once.
  - Or keep the previous version's cache for one generation (delete it only when no client of that version remains).
  - Or don't `skipWaiting` while other clients are open.

**V8: `minted` records are keyed by trait only, so a v2 contract would never be offered.** Owner: app.
- **Where:** `app/src/mint.mjs:52` and `app/src/passport.mjs:83` use `minted[trait.id]` and ignore the stored `contract`/`chainId` (`citizen.mjs:40-45`).
- **Effect:** every reader who sent a v1 mint would see "sent" against a v2 contract, which blocks the migration in "Bot minting: options".
- **Fix:** treat a record as current only when `rec.contract === config(app).address && rec.chainId === config(app).chainId`.

**V9: Sharing a passport while signed out publishes the wallet address `eth_accounts` captured silently.** Owner: app.
- **Where:**
  - `app/src/mint.mjs:92-99` stores `eth_accounts[0]` with no prompt whenever a trait card is shown with a connected wallet.
  - `app/src/view-passport.mjs:66-67` appends `#/p/<that address>` to the share link. The Share button is offered when signed out too (`passport.mjs` actions).
- **Effect:** posting the cast publicly ties the person's Farcaster account to that wallet, without saying so.
- **Fix:** include the address only when it is one of the signed-in fid's verifications, or ask ("include your stamps link (shows your wallet address)?").

**V10: The relay picks the QR / "open on this phone" target, and any https host is accepted.** Owner: identity.
- **Where:** `app/src/identity/flows.mjs:204`.
- **Probe:** `scratch/probes/siwf-probe.test.mjs` prints `onUrl got: https://evil.example/phish?x=1 result: ok`.
- **Effect:** a compromised relay, or one swapped in a future refactor, can send the person to any page.
- **Fix:** allow-list the deep-link hosts (`farcaster.xyz`, `warpcast.com`, `client.warpcast.com`) and require `channelToken` in the query.

**V11: The mint tx has no `chainId`, so the network can change between the check and the send.** Owner: stamps.
- **Where:** `app/src/stamps/index.mjs:193`, `{ from, to, data }`.
- **Effect:** `ensureChain` checks the chain, then `eth_requestAccounts` (which can show UI) runs, then the send. If the person switches network in between, the same calldata goes to the same address on another chain. That costs gas, but there's no loss beyond it.
- **Fix:** add `chainId: chain.hex`. MetaMask, Coinbase and Rabby reject a mismatch. Also re-check `eth_chainId` immediately before the send.

**V12: The pressed stamp is not the stamp you get.** Owner: app/stamps.
- **Where:**
  - `app/src/cards.mjs:227` and `app/src/passport.mjs:83` render previews with `tokenId 0`, so they print serial "#0".
  - The holder is `previewHolder()`, which prefers `verifications[0]` over the wallet that will mint (`citizen.mjs:114-120`).
- **Effect:** the seed is `keccak(holder, stampId, tokenId)`, so the minted art (confetti, tilt, ring rotation) and serial always differ from what the card "pressed". `preview()` itself is exact for (holder, stampId, tokenId) (V-M22 killed).
- **Fix:** render previews without a serial (or "#—") and say "preview". Or, after `eth_requestAccounts`, use the minting account and accept that the serial is unknown until the receipt. Once V1's receipt check is in, redraw from the `Stamped` event's tokenId.

**V13: The SIWE `uri` isn't checked, and the test title claims it is.** Owner: identity.
- **Where:** `flows.mjs:258-259` (the library doesn't check it either).
- **Probe:** a message signed for `https://p-u-c.github.io/SomeOtherProject/` with our nonce is accepted. The nonce still prevents replay.

**V15: The identity key is origin-wide.** Owner: identity.
- **Where:** `store.mjs:11`, `capable.identity.v1`.
- **Effect:** every `p-u-c.github.io` project can read (fid, custody, verifications) and write it. The citizen key is already path-scoped (`citizen.mjs:25`).

**V16: Any framing page is treated as a Mini App host.** Owner: identity (doc).
- **Where:** `core.mjs:50-65` and `shell.mjs:120-135`. Meta CSP can't set `frame-ancestors`, and boot auto-signs-in from the framer's `context.user` and uses its "wallet".
- **Effect:** limited by third-party storage partitioning and by identity being display-only. Worth one line in IDENTITY.md.

**V17: EIP-6963 takes the first announcer.** Owner: identity.
- **Where:** `flows.mjs:302-331`.
- **Effect:** with two wallets installed, which one answers is arbitrary, and the wallet's `info` (name/rdns) is never shown. Spoofing needs an extension in the page, which already owns it, so this is UX.
- **Fix:** a picker, or show `info.name` on the mint button.

**V18: Ownership operations.** Owner: stamps (ops).
- The owner is the deployer EOA `0xF606…98D1`.
- `renounceOwnership()` is single-step and **freezes the current open/closed state forever** (A8). That removes the close lever described in "Bot minting: options".
- **Fix:** move ownership to a Safe or hardware key with `transferOwnership` + `acceptOwnership`. In DEPLOY-STAMPS §7, say "never renounce while any trait is open".

**V19: Burn + re-mint rerolls the art and uses up ids (A3).** Owner: stamps (info).
- Anyone can reroll their seed (confetti and tilt) for about 115k gas per try. Exhausting the supply is infeasible (see A3). Fine as designed; mention it in STAMPS.md.

**V20: `owned()` makes 1 + N parallel eth_calls (up to 257) to the public RPC.** Owner: stamps.
- **Where:** `stamps/index.mjs:136-138`.
- **Effect:** risks 429s from `mainnet.base.org`. Duplicate token ids from a bad RPC pass through (harmless: the UI keys by stampId).
- **Fix:** read `hasStamp(addr, id)` per known trait (4 calls), or batch with JSON-RPC arrays.
- **Robustness verified** (`scratch/probes/owned-probe.mjs`): non-JSON, numbers, error objects (including an HTML payload), empty code, a huge declared array length, a dirty `uint8` and a `uint256` max id all give `[]` plus a reason, and it never throws.

**V21: A deep link longer than about 1,200 chars draws a blank QR.** Owner: identity/app.
- **Where:** `qrSvg` reaches 78 KB at 1,500 chars, and `svgMarkup` caps at 64 KB (`html.mjs:76`), while `validHttpsUrl` allows 2,048 chars.
- **Effect:** the link button still works; real relay links are about 100 chars.

**V22: A stuck deploy is never re-dispatched.** Owner: ingest.
- **Where:** `deploy-if-behind.mjs` treats `queued/pending/in_progress` as up to date.
- **Effect:** a deployment status stuck there (a runner died) is never re-dispatched for that head.
- **Fix:** add an age bound (for example, older than 30 min means dispatch).

**V23: The embed's splash image.** Owner: app.
- `fc:miniapp` uses `icon-192.<hash>.png` as `splashImageUrl`. The spec asks for 200×200, and the hashed URL goes dead in hosts' cached embeds after an icon change.
- **Fix:** an unhashed `splash.png` at 200×200. `embed.png` itself is 1200×800 (3:2), 346 KB, unhashed and not precached.

**V24: Docs drift.** Owner: planner/stamps/identity.
- `docs/STAMPS.md:132` still says `address: null`.
- `STAMPS.md:163` says "app/build.mjs bundles without splitting".
- `docs/IDENTITY.md:123,173` say 600 KB, while SPEC-V3 and `build.mjs:44` say 900 KB.

**V25: Anyone with write access can add commits to an open editor PR, and they auto-merge.** Owner: ingest.
- **Where:** `checkPull` checks only the PR opener (`editor-check.mjs:82`).
- **Fix:** together with V6's bot account, require every commit's `author.login`/`committer.login` (from `GET /pulls/N/commits`) to equal `EDITOR_LOGIN`.

### Checked and fine

**Contract:**
- `approve`, `setApprovalForAll`, `transferFrom` and the 4-arg `safeTransferFrom` are pure reverts. The 3-arg form routes to the 4-arg one, and `_update` refuses owner→owner moves.
- No payable functions, no fallback.
- `_held` and the `tokensOf` swap-and-pop are correct. `uint16` index ≤ 256 entries.
- The seed is `abi.encodePacked` of fixed-size types, so no collisions.
- `Locked` is emitted at mint. `locked`, `stampOf` and `seedOf` revert for missing tokens.
- ERC-165 reports exactly 165/721/721-metadata/5192 (`0xb45a3c0e`).
- The owner can't mint for others, burn, move or edit.
- The trait cap is 256.

**Identity:**
- Nonce: 24 chars from `getRandomValues` with rejection sampling.
- The relay can't swap the nonce, and our own nonce, domain and fid re-checks are killed by mutations.
- Auth-address signers are not stored as custody.
- Storage validation: bidi/control chars, credentialed pfp URLs, the 32 KB cap.
- The pfp is rendered with `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` through `safeUrl`.
- Mini App context is used only for display and the portrait seed, never for minting.

**Mint:**
- `value` is never set. The calldata is `mint(uint8)` to the configured address.
- A 4001 at any step maps to `rejected`. Only `rejected/wrong-chain/offline/no-account` offer a retry. In-flight dedupe.
- `#/p/<addr>` is regex-checked, lowercased and escaped. Public passport art uses the chain's (holder, stampId, tokenId), so it matches on-chain exactly.

**Workflows:**
- No `pull_request_target`, and no `${{ }}` inside `run:`.
- `persist-credentials: false` everywhere. The build job is read-only and the Pages job runs only `deploy-pages`.
- The merge job runs only main's code and merges with `sha=$HEAD_SHA`.
- Fork PRs fail three ways: the job `if`, `head_repository`, and an empty `pull_requests[]`.
- Path rules are robust. Probe in `scratch/gitprobe`: a gitlink (`160000`) and a path with an embedded newline are refused, and symlinks and executables are covered by the existing tests. `--no-ext-diff --no-renames -z`.
- No code-execution sinks fed by lesson JSON in ingest or build.

**CSP:**
- `connect-src` adds exactly the relay, Optimism and Base RPC. `img-src https:` is for pfps.
- viem's CCIP-read fetch during ERC-6492/1271 verification is blocked by `connect-src`.

## Bot minting: options

The constraints:
- v1 at `0xdDe6…C692` is immutable.
- The app is static and serverless, and reading happens in the browser, so nothing on chain can prove someone read a lesson.
- The owner's only lever on v1 is `setOpen` per trait. Owner powers can't remove bot tokens, by design.

The options:

1. **Close the v1 traits (`setOpen(i,false)`).**
   - Stops all new mints: bots and readers alike.
   - The existing bot tokens stay.
   - The app breaks: it still offers the mint, and the txs revert and are recorded as "sent" (V1).
   - **Only after V1's `open` check ships**, and only as a pause while a v2 is prepared.
2. **v2 with mint vouchers (EIP-712) from an off-chain signer that verifies Farcaster identity.**
   - A small serverless function (for example a Cloudflare Worker) verifies a SIWF message or a Farcaster Quick Auth token, then signs `(fid, holder, stampId, deadline)`. The contract keys the one-per rule by **fid** and by holder.
   - Effective against wallet farming: each stamp now costs a Farcaster account, not a wallet. Accounts cost a few dollars and are themselves farmable, so add a per-fid rate limit and optionally an account-age or score threshold.
   - Costs:
     - It breaks "no server".
     - It adds a hot signing key. If compromised, the attacker gets unlimited mints; the owner can rotate the signer.
     - The signer must not run inside an agent session (AGENTS.md: agents hold no keys).
     - It still doesn't prove reading.
   - **The only option with real teeth.**
3. **v2 on Optimism that checks a SIWF custody signature against `IdRegistry.idOf` on chain.**
   - Serverless and trustless, one per fid.
   - Few readers hold their custody key in a browser wallet: it lives in the Farcaster app, and Mini App host wallets are verified addresses, not custody.
   - Rebuilding the SIWE string on chain is fiddly, and stamps would move off Base.
   - Elegant on paper, but the UX blocks it.
4. **EAS gating (for example, require a Coinbase Verified Account attestation on Base).**
   - Serverless and strong.
   - KYC-linked, it excludes most readers, and it contradicts the app's sovereignty and privacy line.
   - Not recommended.
5. **Proof-of-read quiz or answer hash.**
   - In a static app the answers ship in the bundle or sit on chain, so bots read them.
   - Friction for people, theatre for bots. **No.**
6. **A small mint fee in v2.**
   - Deters only marginal farmers and breaks "free".
   - Weak.

**Recommendation:**
- **Now:**
  1. Keep v1 open as an explicitly **self-claimed** stamp (the README already says "honor system").
  2. Change SPEC-V3's "so anyone can verify it" to "anyone can see it".
  3. Never publish holder counts.
  4. Fix V1 and V8 so the owner *can* close traits without breaking readers.
  5. Move ownership off the deployer EOA and never renounce (V18).
- **Later:** only if a stamp must mean "one person", ship option 2 as `CapableStampsV2`:
  - fid-keyed vouchers, minimal signer, rotating key;
  - then close the v1 traits and point `app.meta.json` at v2;
  - the passport can show v1 stamps as "early", read-only.
- Treat bot holders as noise. They cost the project nothing and don't touch real readers' passports.
